# Kotoba Dōjō 言葉道場

*Learn Japanese through aikido.* — *Kotoba* (言葉) means "words": this is a training hall for words, where you practise Japanese the way you practise technique.

You already know more Japanese than you think. Every class you hear *onegai shimasu*, *shihō-nage*, *katate-dori*, *ichi, ni, san, shi*. These lessons start from those sounds and use them to teach you the writing systems, how words are built, and real grammar.

## Who this is for

You train aikido and know dojo words **by sound**, but you can't read Japanese yet. No prior study needed.

## How to use these lessons

1. **One lesson per week of training.** Read it before class.
2. **Listen in class.** Each lesson ends with a *On the mat* task — something to notice or say during training.
3. **Write by hand.** Copy each new hiragana and kanji 5–10 times. Your hand remembers what your eyes forget — like *kata*.
4. **Do the exercises**, then check the answer key at the bottom of each lesson.
5. **Review the glossary** in the changing room. Cover a column and test yourself.

## Conventions

- Romaji (Latin letters) uses macrons for long vowels: **ō**, **ū**. Most dojos write *dojo*, *kokyu*, *ikkyo* — the real words are *dōjō*, *kokyū*, *ikkyō*. Lesson 1 explains why this matters.
- Hyphens split compound technique names for readability: *shihō-nage*, *katate-dori*. Japanese itself uses no spaces.
- Dojo usage varies between styles (Aikikai, Iwama, Yoshinkan, Ki Society…). Where terms differ, the lessons give the most common form and note alternatives.

## After lesson 8

You'll be able to read all of hiragana, recognise ~40 kanji, and understand the grammar behind every phrase you hear in class. Good next steps: learn katakana (the second phonetic script), then pick up a general beginner textbook — *Genki I* or *Minna no Nihongo* — and you'll find the first chapters familiar.
