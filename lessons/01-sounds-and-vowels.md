# Lesson 1 — Sounds and the five vowels

> **Goal:** pronounce dojo words correctly, understand Japanese's three writing systems, and read your first five letters.

## 1. Japanese has only five vowels

Every vowel is short, clean and never changes — unlike English, where "a" can sound five different ways.

| Vowel | Sounds like | Dojo example |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| **a** | *a* in "f**a**ther" | **a**iki, w**a**z**a** |
| **i** | *ee* in "m**ee**t" (short) | **i**rimi, k**i** |
| **u** | *oo* in "f**oo**d" but lips relaxed, not rounded | **u**ke, **u**ra |
| **e** | *e* in "g**e**t" | uk**e**, nag**e**, s**e**nsei |
| **o** | *o* in "**o**rder" (short) | **o**mote, k**o**te |

Practice: say **a-i-u-e-o** out loud. That's the order Japanese uses — the "alphabet order" of the language.

**Every syllable ends in a vowel** (or the sound *n*). That's why *ki* + *ai* = *kiai*, and why Japanese turns borrowed words into *bīru* (beer) or *terebi* (TV).

## 2. Long vowels — the part dojos get wrong

A **long vowel** is held twice as long. In romaji it's written with a line (macron): **ō**, **ū**.

Most dojos drop the line. That hides the real word:

| What you see | What it really is | Count the beats |
|--------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| dojo | **dōjō** | do-o-jo-o (4 beats) |
| kokyu | **kokyū** | ko-kyu-u (3 beats) |
| ikkyo | **ikkyō** | i-k-kyo-o (4 beats) |
| aikido | **aikidō** | a-i-ki-do-o (5 beats) |
| shiho-nage | **shihō-nage** | shi-ho-o-na-ge |

This is not pedantry: length changes meaning. *Ojisan* = uncle, *ojiisan* = grandfather. *Kyō* = today / teaching, *kyo* = emptiness.

> Japanese counts **beats** (called *mora*), not syllables. Each beat takes the same time — like a metronome during *suburi*.

## 3. Consonant notes

- **r** is a quick tap of the tongue — between *r*, *l* and *d*. *Rei* sounds a bit like "day" with a soft flick.
- **tsu** as in "ca**ts**". *Tsuki* is not "suki"!
- **fu** is softer than English f — blow gently, lips barely touching: *fu*.
- **Double consonants** (*kk*, *pp*, *tt*) mean a tiny pause, like a held breath before a strike: *i-k-kyō*, *ka-t-te*.
- **n** on its own is a full beat: *te-n-ka-n* is 4 beats.
- **u and i often go silent** between voiceless consonants: *shimasu* sounds like "shimas", *desu* like "des".

## 4. Three writing systems

Japanese mixes three scripts in the same sentence:

| Script | What it is | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Hiragana** ひらがな | Phonetic, curvy. Grammar endings and native words. | あいきどう (aikidō) |
| **Katakana** カタカナ | Phonetic, angular. Foreign loanwords. | アイキドー (rarely used for aikidō) |
| **Kanji** 漢字 | Characters from Chinese; each carries meaning. | 合気道 (aikidō) |

Normal writing: **合気道の稽古をします** — "(I) do aikido practice". Kanji for the meaning, hiragana for the grammar.

### And romaji?

The words in this lesson, like *aikidō* or *shihō-nage*, are written in **romaji** (ローマ字, "Roman letters"): Japanese spelled with the Latin alphabet. It is **not** a fourth Japanese script. Japanese people use it for signs, passports and typing on a keyboard (`aikidou` → あいきどう → 合気道), but never to write Japanese in books or messages.

Romaji is a useful bridge, but it can mislead you:
- **Long vowels disappear**: *dojo* is really *dōjō*.
- **There are several spelling systems**: these lessons use **Hepburn** (*shi*, *tsu*, *fu*). Others write *si*, *tu*, *hu* for the same sounds.
- **It invites English pronunciation**: *uke* is *oo-keh*, not "yoo-kay".

Use romaji to get started, then let it go. By lesson 5 you'll read hiragana instead.

We start with **hiragana**: 46 basic characters, each one beat. Learn it and you can write *any* Japanese word.

## 5. Reading corner: あ い う え お

Your first five letters — and the first word is already in them.

| Kana | Sound | Memory hook |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **あ** | a | A cross with a loop — someone in **a**n *aikidō* stance |
| **い** | i | Two strokes, like two **ee**ls — or uke and nage side by side |
| **う** | u | A person bowing with a hat on — "**oo**f!" after a fall |
| **え** | e | A kneeling figure — **e**nergy before standing |
| **お** | o | Like あ with an extra dot — "**o**h, a dot!" |

Now read:

- **あい** = *ai* — "harmony / joining", the first half of *aikidō*
- **うえ** = *ue* — "up / above"
- **いえ** = *ie* — "house"
- **あおい** = *aoi* — "blue"

Write each character 10 times. Stroke order: top to bottom, left to right. See the [hiragana chart](../reference/hiragana-chart.md).

## Exercises

1. Rewrite with correct long vowels: *dojo*, *kokyu-ho*, *nikyo*, *budo*.
2. How many beats in *tenkan*? In *ikkyō*?
3. Read aloud: **いう**, **おい**, **あえ**, **うえ**.
4. Write in hiragana: *ai*, *ue*, *ie*.

## On the mat

During class, listen to your teacher saying *dōjō*, *kokyū-hō* or *ikkyō*. Can you hear the long vowel? Japanese teachers always make it; non-Japanese teachers often don't.
