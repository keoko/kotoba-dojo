# Lesson 3 — Counting and the body

> **Goal:** count warm-ups to ten, name body parts and directions, use the particle の, and finish the basic hiragana chart.

## 1. Counting the warm-up

| # | Dojo count | Hiragana | Alternative |
|---|-----------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | ichi | いち | |
| 2 | ni | に | |
| 3 | san | さん | |
| 4 | **shi** | し | **yon** よん |
| 5 | go | ご | |
| 6 | roku | ろく | |
| 7 | **shichi** | しち | **nana** なな |
| 8 | hachi | はち | |
| 9 | **ku** | く | **kyū** きゅう |
| 10 | jū | じゅう | |

Why two words for 4, 7 and 9? Japanese has native and Chinese-origin numbers mixed together. *Shi* also sounds like 死 "death", and *ku* like 苦 "suffering", so in daily life Japanese people often prefer *yon*, *nana*, *kyū*. In counting rhythms (like warm-ups) the short forms *shi*, *shichi*, *ku* are common.

This is why the 4th technique is **yon**kyō, not *shikyō*.

Beyond ten, it's like building blocks: 11 = *jū-ichi* (ten-one), 20 = *ni-jū* (two-ten), 35 = *san-jū-go*.

### Three kinds of "front"

English says "front" for all of these. Japanese has a different word for each:

| Word | Kanji | Means | Example |
|------|-------|-------|---------|
| **mae** | 前 | the space **in front of** someone or something; also **before** in time | *mae ukemi*, roll forward |
| **omote** | 表 | the **front side** of a thing, its surface | *ikkyō omote*, entering on uke's front side |
| **shōmen** | 正面 | the **face-on front**: the front of the dojo, or the front of the head | *shōmen ni rei*, *shōmen-uchi* |

*Mae* and *ushiro* are a pair, like *omote* and *ura*: **mae ukemi** (forward roll) and **ushiro ukemi** (backward roll).

## 4. Grammar: **の** (*no*) — "'s / of"

**の** links two nouns: **A の B** = "A's B" / "the B of A".

- **aite no te** — partner's hand
- **sensei no waza** — the teacher's technique
- **migi no te** — the right hand (lit. "the hand of the right")
- **uke no kata** — uke's shoulder
- **sensei no mae** — in front of the teacher (lit. "the teacher's front")
- **keiko no mae** — before practice (*mae* also works for time)

Order matters: the owner comes **first**. *Te no aite* would mean "the hand's partner" — nonsense.

Compare with **に** from lesson 2:
- **sensei ni** — *to* the teacher
- **sensei no** — the teacher*'s*

With lesson 1 and 2 that's **all 46 basic hiragana**. Don't panic — learn one row a day. The [chart](../reference/hiragana-chart.md) has them all.

Special notes:
- **は** is read **wa** when it's the topic particle ("as for…"). *Konnichi**wa*** is written こんにち**は**.
- **を** is only used as the object particle (*o*): *keiko **o** shimasu* → けいこ**を**します.
- **ぬ / め**, **ね / れ / わ**, **る / ろ** look alike. Compare them side by side.

Memory hooks:
- **ぬ** (nu) — noodles with chopsticks.
- **ふ** (fu) — Mount **Fu**ji with clouds.
- **め** (me) — an **eye** — and *me* means eye!
- **も** (mo) — a fish hook with **mo**re bait.
- **ろ** (ro) — a **ro**ad that bends; **る** (ru) has a loop at the end — "**ru**n around".

## Exercises

1. Count aloud from 1 to 10, then 10 to 1, using *yon / nana / kyū* the second time.
2. Say in Japanese: 15, 22, 40.
3. Translate: *sensei no hakama*, *uke no kubi*, *hidari no ashi*.
4. Read: **なまえ**, **ひと**, **ゆめ**, **よる**, **わたし**, **さむらい**.
5. Write in hiragana: *omote*, *ura*, *irimi*, *mune*, *hara*.

## On the mat

During warm-ups, count silently along with whoever leads. Notice whether your dojo says *shi* or *yon*, *shichi* or *nana*.
