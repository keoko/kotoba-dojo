# Lesson 7 — Commands on the mat

> **Goal:** understand the instructions you hear in class and learn three essential verb forms.

## 1. Short commands

| Command | Hiragana | Kanji | Meaning | Grammar |
|---------|----------|-------|---------|---------|
| **Hajime!** | はじめ | 始め | Begin! | stem of *hajimeru* (to begin) |
| **Yame!** | やめ | 止め | Stop! | stem of *yameru* (to stop) |
| **Mō ichido** | もういちど | もう一度 | Once more | *mō* (more) + *ichi* (one) + *do* (time) |
| **Kōtai** | こうたい | 交代 | Switch (roles) | noun: "alternation" |
| **Ki o tsuke!** | きをつけ | 気をつけ | Attention! | lit. "attach your *ki*" |
| **Seiza** | せいざ | 正座 | Sit formally | noun used as command |
| **Hai** | はい | — | Yes / understood | |

Two patterns here:
- **The bare stem as a sharp command**: *hajime*, *yame*. Like lesson 4 — the stem is a noun, and shouted it becomes an order. Casual and abrupt; perfect for a dojo.
- **Ki o tsuke** uses **を** (*o*), the object marker: "*ki* (object) attach". In everyday Japanese **気をつけて** *ki o tsukete* means "take care!" — literally "attach your attention".

## 2. Three verb forms

Every Japanese verb has a few forms. These three cover most of what you'll hear.

| Dictionary form | -masu form (polite) | -te form | Meaning |
|-----------------|---------------------|----------|---------|
| **suru** する | shimasu | **shite** して | do |
| **miru** みる | mimasu | **mite** みて | look, watch |
| **tatsu** たつ | tachimasu | **tatte** たって | stand |
| **suwaru** すわる | suwarimasu | **suwatte** すわって | sit |
| **yaru** やる | yarimasu | **yatte** やって | do (casual) |
| **hajimeru** はじめる | hajimemasu | **hajimete** はじめて | begin |
| **nageru** なげる | nagemasu | **nagete** なげて | throw |
| **ukeru** うける | ukemasu | **ukete** うけて | receive |
| **kaeru** かえる (*go home*) | kaerimasu | **kaette** かえって | return home |

- **Dictionary form** — the "plain" form, used with friends and in dictionaries.
- **-masu form** — the polite form (lesson 2).
- **-te form** — the connector. Its most useful job is the next pattern.

The -te form rules are a little irregular; for now, **learn the dojo verbs as chunks**. A general textbook will give you the full rules later and you'll already know the examples.

## 3. **-te kudasai** — "please do…"

**[-te form] + kudasai** = a polite request.

| Japanese | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Mite kudasai.** | Please watch. |
| **Tatte kudasai.** | Please stand up. |
| **Suwatte kudasai.** | Please sit down. |
| **Mō ichido shite kudasai.** | Please do it once more. |
| **Yukkuri shite kudasai.** | Please do it slowly. |
| **Tsuyoku nigitte kudasai.** | Please grip strongly. |

*Kudasai* comes from "please give (me)". So *mite kudasai* is literally "give me the favour of watching".

## 4. **-mashō** — "let's…"

Change **-masu** to **-mashō** to say "let's":

| -masu | -mashō | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| hajimemasu | **hajimemashō** | Let's begin |
| shimasu | **shimashō** | Let's do it |
| yarimasu | **yarimashō** | Let's do it (casual verb, polite ending) |
| kōtai shimasu | **kōtai shimashō** | Let's switch |

## 5. Putting it together: a teacher's instructions

> **Mite kudasai.** *Katate-dori ikkyō omote.* **Hai, hajime!**
> … … …
> **Yame!** **Kōtai shite kudasai.** **Mō ichido.**

"Please watch. One-hand grab, first teaching, front form. OK, begin! … Stop! Please switch. Once more."

## Exercises

1. Make polite requests: "please stand", "please watch", "please throw".
2. Change to "let's": *keiko o shimasu*, *hajimemasu*.
3. What is the difference between *Yame!* and *Yamete kudasai*?
4. Translate: *Mō ichido shite kudasai.*
5. Read and translate: **たってください**, **はじめましょう**, **きをつけ**.

## On the mat

Ask your partner **"Mō ichido onegai shimasu"** — "Once more, please" — when you want to repeat a technique.
