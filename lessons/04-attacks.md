# Lesson 4 — Attacks

> **Goal:** understand how attack names are built, learn how verbs become nouns, and learn the voicing marks ゛ and ゜.

## 1. Attacks are verbs turned into nouns

Japanese makes a noun from a verb by taking its **stem** — the part before *-masu*.

| Verb (dictionary) | Polite | Stem = noun | Meaning |
|-------------------|--------|-------------|---------|
| **utsu** 打つ | uchimasu | **uchi** | strike |
| **tsuku** 突く | tsukimasu | **tsuki** | thrust / punch |
| **toru** 取る | torimasu | **tori** | grab / take |
| **nageru** 投げる | nagemasu | **nage** | throw / thrower |
| **ukeru** 受ける | ukemasu | **uke** | receive / receiver |
| **kaesu** 返す | kaeshimasu | **kaeshi** | turn over / return |

So **uke** is literally "the receiving one", **nage** "the throwing one", **tori** "the taking one". The roles *are* verbs.

> Trick: you can find the stem of any polite verb by cutting *-masu*. *Uchi-masu* → *uchi*.

## 2. Building attack names

Attack names are **compounds**: [where / with what] + [action].

| Attack | Parts | Literal |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **shōmen-uchi** | shōmen (front face) + uchi | front-of-head strike |
| **yokomen-uchi** | yoko (side) + men (face) + uchi | side-of-head strike |
| **chūdan-tsuki** | chūdan (middle level) + tsuki | middle punch (see section 3) |
| **jōdan-tsuki** | jōdan (upper level) + tsuki | upper punch, to the face |
| **katate-dori** | kata (one of a pair) + te (hand) + **d**ori | one-hand grab |
| **ryōte-dori** | ryō (both) + te + dori | both-hands grab (each of nage's wrists) |
| **morote-dori** | moro (both) + te + dori | two hands grabbing one arm |
| **kata-dori** | kata (shoulder) + dori | shoulder grab |
| **mune-dori** | mune (chest) + dori | lapel grab |
| **ushiro ryōtekubi-dori** | behind + both + hand-neck (wrist) + dori | both wrists grabbed from behind |

Note **tekubi**: *te* (hand) + *kubi* (neck) = **wrist**. The wrist is "the hand's neck". Japanese builds words like Lego.

Also note **katate** (one hand) vs **kata** (shoulder) — different words that start the same.

### Combined attacks: kata-dori men-uchi

Attack names can be **stacked**, in the order things happen:

| Attack | Kanji | Hiragana | Literal |
|--------|-------|----------|---------|
| **kata-dori men-uchi** | 肩取り面打ち | かたどりめんうち | shoulder grab, face strike |

Uke grabs your shoulder with one hand, then strikes at your head with the other (usually like *shōmen-uchi*). The name is the sequence: first **kata-dori** (肩 shoulder + 取り grab), then **men-uchi** (面 face/head + 打ち strike).

**Men** alone means face or head (lesson 3). *Shōmen-uchi* and *yokomen-uchi* just add *where* on the head: 正 straight (the front) or 横 side. *Men-uchi* with nothing in front is simply "head strike".

### Atemi: nage's strikes

Attacks aren't only uke's. **Atemi** 当身 is a strike *by nage*, usually to unbalance uke, open a line or take their attention, more than to hurt.

| Part | Kanji | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| **ate** | 当 | hit, touch the target (from 当てる *ateru*, "to hit, to apply") |
| **mi** | 身 | body (as in *irimi*, *ukemi*) |

Literally "hitting the body". The verb pattern is the same as before: 当てる *ateru* → stem *ate*, and the noun is built from it.

## 3. Three levels: jōdan, chūdan, gedan

Attacks and stances are often named by the **height** they target. Each is a direction + **dan** 段 ("step, level"):

| Level | Kanji | Literal | Target | Dojo use |
|-------|-------|---------|--------|----------|
| **jōdan** | 上段 | upper step | head, face | *jōdan-tsuki* (punch to the face) |
| **chūdan** | 中段 | middle step | body, stomach | *chūdan-tsuki* (punch to the body), the most common *tsuki* in aikido |
| **gedan** | 下段 | lower step | below the belt | *gedan no kamae* (low sword or *jō* stance) |

Three things to notice:

- **段 is the same kanji as your rank.** *Shodan*, *nidan*… are "first step, second step" (lesson 8). A black belt is literally climbing steps.
- **上 and 下 change their reading.** Alone they're *ue* (up) and *shita* (down) from lesson 3. In compounds they switch to their Chinese-origin readings *jō* and *ge*. Lesson 6 explains these two kinds of reading.
- **中** (*chū*, middle) is a picture: a line through the middle of a box. Alone it's read *naka*, "inside, middle".

When a teacher just says ***tsuki***, it usually means *chūdan-tsuki*. And *tsuki* has a homophone: 月 *tsuki* means **moon**.

## 4. Rendaku: why *tori* becomes *dori*

When *tori* is the second part of a compound, it becomes **dori**. This voicing is called **rendaku** ("sequential voicing"). It happens a lot:

| Alone | In a compound |
|-------|---------------|
| tori | katate-**d**ori |
| kaeshi | kote-**g**aeshi |
| tachi (standing) | hanmi han-**d**achi |
| te (hand) | kata-te — no change |

Rendaku is common but not a strict rule, so learn each compound as you meet it. It is the reason you need the marks in the next section.

## 5. Reading corner: ゛ and ゜

Two small marks change a consonant's sound.

**Dakuten ゛** (two dots) — makes the sound "voiced":

| k → g | s → z | t → d | h → b |
|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| が ga | ざ za | だ da | ば ba |
| ぎ gi | じ **ji** | ぢ ji (rare) | び bi |
| ぐ gu | ず zu | づ zu (rare) | ぶ bu |
| げ ge | ぜ ze | で de | べ be |
| ご go | ぞ zo | ど do | ぼ bo |

**Handakuten ゜** (small circle) — only on the h-row, makes **p**:

| ぱ pa | ぴ pi | ぷ pu | ぺ pe | ぽ po |

Now you can read rendaku in action:

| Hiragana | Romaji |
|----------|--------|
| とり → かたて**ど**り | tori → katate-**d**ori |
| かえし → こて**が**えし | kaeshi → kote-**g**aeshi |

More words:

| Hiragana | Romaji | Meaning |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **なげ** | nage | throw / thrower |
| **わざ** | waza | technique |
| **うち** | uchi | strike |
| **つき** | tsuki | thrust |
| **げだん** | gedan | lower level |
| **かたてどり** | katate-dori | one-hand grab |
| **かたどり** | kata-dori | shoulder grab |
| **むねどり** | mune-dori | chest grab |
| **よこめんうち** | yokomen-uchi | side strike |
| **こてがえし** | kote-gaeshi | wrist turn-out |
| **ぶどう** | budō | martial way — **おう** is a long *ō* (lesson 5) |
| **どうぎ** | dōgi | training uniform — long *ō* again |
| **すわりわざ** | suwari-waza | kneeling techniques |
| **ぼっけん** | bokken | wooden sword (small っ — next lesson!) |

## Exercises

1. Give the stem (noun form) of: *nagemasu*, *uchimasu*, *torimasu*, *kaeshimasu*.
2. What does *ryōte-dori* literally mean? *Morote-dori*?
3. Why is it *kote-gaeshi* and not *kote-kaeshi*?
4. Read: **ごはん**, **かぜ**, **でんわ**, **ぶき**, **じかん**, **ぱん**.
5. Write in hiragana: *nage*, *waza*, *mune-dori*, *budō*, *kata-dori*.
6. Where does a *jōdan-tsuki* go? A *chūdan-tsuki*? What does *dan* 段 mean, and where else do you hear it in the dojo?
7. Break down *kata-dori men-uchi*: what happens first, and what second? Write it in hiragana.

## On the mat

When your teacher names the attack, say the literal meaning to yourself: "one-hand-grab", "side-face-strike". Notice the attack *is* its name.
