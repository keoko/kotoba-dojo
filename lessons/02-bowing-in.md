# Lesson 2 — Bowing in

> **Goal:** understand every word of the opening and closing ceremony, learn polite verb endings, and read 16 more hiragana.

## 1. The phrases, taken apart

### Onegai shimasu — おねがいします

Said before practice and when bowing to a partner.

| Part | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **o-** | Polite prefix ("honourable") |
| **negai** | A wish / request (from *negau*, to wish) |
| **shimasu** | "(I) do" — polite form of *suru* |

Literally: *"I make a (humble) request."* — i.e. "Please (train with me / teach me)". The longer form is **yoroshiku onegai shimasu**, "please treat me well".

### Arigatō gozaimashita — ありがとうございました

Said at the end of class and to each partner.

| Part | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **arigatō** | Thanks (originally "it is rare / precious") |
| **gozaimashita** | Very polite "was" — **past tense** |

Why past? Because you are thanking for something **finished** — the practice that just happened. Before help, Japanese says *arigatō gozaimasu* (present). That one sound — **-masu** vs **-mashita** — is a whole grammar lesson. Keep reading.

### Other dojo phrases

| Romaji | Hiragana | Meaning / literal |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| **hai** | はい | Yes / understood |
| **rei** | れい | Bow (礼, "courtesy") |
| **seiza** | せいざ | Formal kneeling ("correct sitting") |
| **mokusō** | もくそう | Meditation ("silent thought") |
| **shitsurei shimasu** | しつれいします | "Excuse me" — entering/leaving the mat ("I commit a discourtesy") |
| **sensei** | せんせい | Teacher ("one born before") |

### Ways of sitting

| Romaji | Hiragana | Kanji | Literal | What it is |
|--------|----------|-------|---------|------------|
| **seiza** | せいざ | 正座 | correct sitting | Formal kneeling, sitting on your heels, back straight |
| **kiza** | きざ | 跪座 | kneeling sitting | Kneeling with toes curled under, ready to move: for *shikkō* (knee walking) and before standing up |
| **anza** / **agura** | あんざ / あぐら | 安座 / 胡座 | easy sitting | Relaxed cross-legged sitting. Often allowed if seiza hurts your knees |

**Seiza is not zazen.** *Zazen* (坐禅, "sitting Zen") is a **practice**: seated Zen meditation, usually cross-legged on a cushion. *Seiza* is a **posture**. You can do zazen in seiza, but the words mean different things. The dojo's **mokusō** (黙想, "silent thought") is the short meditation done in seiza at the start and end of class. It's closer to zazen, but brief and not formal Zen practice.

Notice **座** (*za*, "sit, seat") in *seiza*, *kiza* and *anza*. You'll meet it again in lesson 8.

## 2. Grammar: polite verbs, -masu and -mashita

Japanese verbs don't change for *I / you / he*. They change for **time** and **politeness**.

| | Present / future | Past |
|-|------------------|------|
| Positive | **-masu** | **-mashita** |
| Negative | -masen | -masen deshita |

With *shimasu* ("do"):

- **shimasu** — I do / will do
- **shimashita** — I did
- **shimasen** — I don't / won't
- **keiko o shimasu** — I (will) practise
- **keiko o shimashita** — I practised

Note: Japanese drops the subject when it's obvious. *Shimashita* alone is a complete sentence: "(I) did (it)."

## 3. Grammar: the particle **に** (*ni*) — "to / toward"

Listen to the opening ceremony:

| Command | Literal | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Shōmen ni rei** | front **to** bow | Bow to the front (the *kamiza*, the shrine/photo of O-Sensei; see lesson 8) |
| **Sensei ni rei** | teacher **to** bow | Bow to the teacher |
| **Otagai ni rei** | each-other **to** bow | Bow to each other |

**ni** comes *after* the word it marks — Japanese particles are like tags stuck on the end. *Sensei ni* = "to the teacher". Compare English, where "to" comes first.

> Japanese word order: **[who/what] + particle … verb at the end.** The verb always comes last.

## 4. Reading corner: か・さ・た rows and ん

Hiragana is a grid: a consonant + each of the five vowels.

| | a | i | u | e | o |
|-|---|---|---|---|---|
| **k** | か ka | き ki | く ku | け ke | こ ko |
| **s** | さ sa | し **shi** | す su | せ se | そ so |
| **t** | た ta | ち **chi** | つ **tsu** | て te | と to |

Watch the irregular ones: **し shi**, **ち chi**, **つ tsu**.

Plus one special letter:

| **ん** | n | The only consonant that stands alone. Always one beat. |

### Memory hooks

Read the hook while looking at the letter, then write it once. A silly picture you *see* sticks better than a clever one you only read, so if a hook doesn't work for you, make your own.

| Kana | Sound | Hook |
|------|-------|------|
| **か** | ka | A **k** with an extra slash beside it: the cut of a ***ka**tana* |
| **き** | ki | A **key**: *ki* is the key to aikido |
| **く** | ku | A bent wrist, as in *nikyō*. Or a bird's beak calling "**ku**-ku" |
| **け** | ke | Uke (the left stroke) standing beside a *jō* held crosswise. It's the last letter of う**け** |
| **こ** | ko | Two short strokes: two **co**llarbones, or two **ko**i fish |
| **さ** | sa | A **sa**murai bowing: き with one bar gone, as if the samurai lowered their sword |
| **し** | shi | A fish hook: "**she** caught a fish" |
| **す** | su | A *bokken* swing with a loop of follow-through: ***su**buri* |
| **せ** | se | The first letter of **せ**んせい: a teacher's long arm pointing at the technique |
| **そ** | so | A zig-zag path, like stepping in and turning: "**so**, that's how you move" |
| **た** | ta | It looks like the letters **t** + **a** written together. Also the first letter of **た**たみ |
| **ち** | chi | A person with a big belly: a strong centre, full of energy ("**chi**") |
| **つ** | tsu | A wave: **tsu**nami |
| **て** | te | A hand reaching out, and *te* means **hand** |
| **と** | to | A **toe** with a splinter in it |

**Look-alikes to watch:**
- **き ki** has two bars and **さ sa** has one.
- **さ sa** and **ち chi** are mirror images: さ's bottom curve opens to the right (like a **c**), and ち's opens to the left (like a **5**).
- **し shi** hooks to the right, and **つ tsu** is a wave lying on its side.
- In printed fonts, **き** and **さ** often have a separate bottom stroke. By hand it's usually joined. Both are correct.

| **て** | te | hand |
| **うけ** | uke | the one who receives (and falls) |
| **こて** | kote | wrist |
